# Zoom Panel Roadmap - 21-27 July 2026

## Mission

Be ready for the 60-minute AI Deployment Strategist panel on **Monday 27 July 2026 at 20:00 BST**. The objective is not to memorise perfect scripts. It is to demonstrate, under follow-up pressure, that Ellis can lead a strategic customer from ambiguous POC to measurable, trusted production use while aligning Sales, Product, Engineering, and operations.

No preparation plan guarantees an interview outcome. In this roadmap, **100% prepared** means every readiness gate below has objective evidence and no critical unknown is being ignored.

## Operating rules

1. Speak answers aloud. Reading and silent drafting do not count as completed practice.
2. Attempt before checking notes. Score the first attempt, record one improvement, then retry on a later day.
3. Keep first answers to 60-120 seconds unless the panel asks for depth.
4. Use one concrete example, one trade-off, one failure path, and one measurable outcome in technical answers.
5. Be precise about ownership: product direction and validation were Ellis-led; substantial code was AI-assisted.
6. Never claim production customers, production telephony, paid use, scale, or unaided implementation for RelayClarity.
7. Stop adding new material after Sunday afternoon. Monday is for recall, composure, and delivery.
8. Protect sleep, food, movement, and voice. Exhaustion reduces recall and judgment.

## The six signals the panel must leave with

- **Customer deployment leader:** can discover the real problem, control scope, define success, and move a POC toward go-live in 90 days.
- **Technical strategist:** can reason across voice, RAG, tools, integrations, security, reliability, evaluation, and operations without hiding behind jargon.
- **Business translator:** connects architecture to resolution, CSAT, customer effort, cost per resolution, adoption, revenue, and time to value.
- **Cross-functional operator:** handles tension between customer urgency, Sales commitments, Engineering capacity, security, and Product priorities.
- **Evidence-led owner:** tests normal and adversarial cases, sets launch gates, observes failures, documents decisions, and improves after launch.
- **Zoom culture fit:** shows Care, empathy, reliability, professionalism, responsiveness, knowledge, follow-up, flexibility, and anticipation of needs.

## Stand-out strategy

Most candidates will describe AI features. Ellis should describe a repeatable **deployment operating model**:

**Discover -> baseline -> scope -> integrate -> configure -> evaluate -> launch gate -> phased release -> observe -> improve -> hand off -> generalise learning**

Stand out by doing the following consistently:

- Reframe containment as insufficient without genuine resolution, low repeat contact, clean handoff, and customer satisfaction.
- Start with the customer's top contact drivers and data readiness before discussing models.
- Treat knowledge quality, permissions, integration failure, human escalation, and operational ownership as first-class design concerns.
- Use current Zoom language naturally: Agent Architect, Agent Performance Suite, shared customer context, Quality Management, cost per resolution, outcome-based pricing, and multi-location deployment.
- Explain what would block launch rather than presenting every problem as solvable through prompt tuning.
- Convert field gaps into a reusable capability, playbook, test, or product requirement.
- Ask panel questions that reveal how this team works, not questions answerable from the website.
- Be candid when experience is simulated or local, then demonstrate the production method that would be used.

Do not force a demo or screen share. Keep a five-minute RelayClarity walkthrough and architecture diagram ready, and use them only if the panel invites it.

## In-interview playbook

### Opening

- Greet the full panel and note names/roles.
- Give a concise introduction, then allow the panel to set direction.
- If the agenda is unclear, ask: "Before we begin, would it help if I briefly frame my background, or would you prefer to go straight into questions?"

### Answer control

1. Pause for one or two seconds.
2. Clarify ambiguous scope.
3. Give the headline answer first.
4. Structure the explanation visibly.
5. Use one example and one trade-off.
6. State how success is measured.
7. Stop and invite follow-up.

Useful recovery lines:

- "Let me separate the customer decision from the implementation detail."
- "The first thing I would validate is..."
- "That would be a launch blocker because..."
- "I have not run that exact component in production; my adjacent evidence is..., and I would de-risk it by..."
- "There are two reasonable options. The choice depends on..."
- "I want to correct one part of my previous answer..."

### Panel presence

- Address the person who asked, then bring gaze back to the full panel.
- Do not interpret neutral faces as negative feedback; panelists may be taking structured notes.
- Do not rush to fill silence.
- Track multi-part questions on paper and answer each part.
- Treat challenge as a collaboration test, not an argument.

### Questions and close

Choose two or three questions based on who attended. Finish with a concise close:

> What excites me about this role is the gap between a compelling AI demonstration and a deployment a customer can trust every day. RelayClarity helped me explore that operating model, and this process has made me more certain that bridging customer outcomes, technical delivery, and reusable product learning is where I add the most value.

Thank the whole panel and confirm the recruiting next step without pressing for an immediate decision.
