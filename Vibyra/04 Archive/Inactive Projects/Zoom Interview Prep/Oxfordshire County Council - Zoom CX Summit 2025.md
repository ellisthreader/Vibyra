# Oxfordshire County Council - Zoom CX Summit 2025

## What the summit coverage reports

- Laura Ball, Zoom CX AI Lead, hosted a session bringing together Oxfordshire County Council and One&Only Resorts to show how very different service environments share a need for customers to feel remembered rather than processed.
- Oxfordshire County Council was represented by Clare Martin and Richard Merritt.
- Clare Martin described the trust context directly: “Our citizens don’t come to us for fun. They come when something’s gone wrong, so trust becomes everything.”
- The council example focused on empowering frontline staff to create small, genuine moments of care. Richard Merritt’s point was that technology matters through how it helps people feel heard, not as an end in itself.

## Why this is useful for the Zoom interview

- It is a public-sector proof point for Laura Ball’s human-centred AI framing: start with the emotional and service context, then place technology where it helps staff respond with empathy.
- Applied to StyleHub WISMO: the stakes are lower than a council crisis, but the journey often starts because something has gone wrong. The design should reduce repetition and anxiety, preserve context at handoff, and give the human agent enough information to respond personally.
- Useful language: “The Oxfordshire example reinforced that automation should create room for care. The customer should feel heard and remembered, not processed.”
- Link to John Aspinall’s connected-journey position: carrying identity, history and intent across channels helps frontline staff continue the conversation instead of restarting it.
